- daily [[habit]]s and actions to [[practice]] for [[goals]]
    - morning habits (start with clarity) 1. set a daily impact intention: • ask: what’s one thing i can do today that will create meaningful progress or connection? focus on an input you control, not the outcome. • write this down and commit to it before starting the day. 2. reframe the day positively: • spend 2 minutes identifying what excites you about today, even small things. this helps counter dread or time anxiety. 3. meditate on imperfection (5 minutes): • repeat a mantra like “my worth is not tied to what I produce” or “failure is feedback.” visualize releasing perfectionist tension. work habits (build momentum) 1. the “80/20 first hour”: • start your workday by spending one hour on the single task that aligns most with your top 20% goals (e.g., creative ideation, impactful planning). avoid emails or shallow work during this time. 2. embrace small wins: • at the end of any work session, take 30 seconds to reflect: what did I learn, create, or improve? consciously celebrate progress, not just outcomes. 3. practice small bets: • daily, take one action where the stakes feel slightly uncomfortable but manageable (e.g., pitching an idea, initiating a vulnerable conversation, experimenting with a new approach). track these risks in a journal. relationship habits (deepen connection) 1. express one small vulnerability: • share something honest but low-stakes with someone close to you, even if it’s just “I felt a little nervous about that” or “I could use your advice.” this builds trust and reduces overthinking. 2. clarify and communicate needs: • if something feels off in a relationship (e.g., with carter), address it promptly and directly. use statements like “I feel X because Y. I’d like to do Z instead.” this prevents resentment from building. 3. practice gratitude or humor in interactions: • intentionally highlight something positive or lighthearted in every conversation. this helps balance your intensity and keeps connections playful. reflection habits (align with growth) 1. nightly “mistake and win” journaling: • write one mistake you made (and what you learned from it) and one win you had (no matter how small). this normalizes imperfection and celebrates progress. 2. control audit: • ask: where did I try to control too much today? reflect on how you could release that grip tomorrow. 3. revisit your goals weekly: • schedule 5 minutes each night to quickly check if your actions align with long-term priorities. adjust as needed. play/rest habits (balance intensity) 1. schedule non-productive time daily: • spend at least 30 minutes on an activity that isn’t tied to achievement (e.g., cooking, gaming, watching a show). let yourself enjoy it without guilt. 2. use micro-moments for joy: • during transitions (e.g., walking, commuting), focus on sensory details around you or recall a positive memory. this trains your brain to find small joys without needing grand achievements. 3. end the day with humor or levity: • watch or read something playful or lighthearted before bed to balance your emotional intensity and reinforce a sense of fun. weekly commitments 1. plan one “stretch goal” conversation or project: • push yourself to try something ambitious weekly—this could be pitching a bold idea at work or sharing a deep vulnerability with someone close. track these and celebrate your courage. 2. reflect on relationships: • assess if any relationships need clearer communication or nurturing. schedule time to connect deeply with someone you value. 3. pause to recalibrate: • spend 15 minutes every sunday reviewing your impact from the past week and resetting priorities for the week ahead. key mindset shifts (reinforced through action) 1. focus on inputs: prioritize the effort you put into tasks, relationships, and growth, not the results you get. 2. redefine failure: treat every mistake as a natural part of the process and an opportunity to refine your approach. 3. value play and rest: acknowledge that downtime isn’t wasted—it’s an essential part of creating sustainable, meaningful work and relationships. these habits create small, repeatable loops that dismantle unhelpful patterns (e.g., overanalysis, paralysis) while building new ones (e.g., action, connection, self-compassion). as you consistently practice them, they’ll reinforce the growth you seek without the weight of perfectionism or fear.
