# RO-DBT Family Relationship Guidance - September 27, 2025

**Situation:** Difficulty with sister relationship affecting both user and father

---

Hello Sam,

I'm taking on the role of your RO-DBT-informed therapeutic guide today. Let me approach this family situation with the radical openness and curiosity that RO-DBT teaches us.

## Therapeutic Assessment

First, let's pause and notice what's happening in your body right now as you think about your sister. Are you feeling tension? Heat? Constriction? These physical sensations are valuable information about where you might be getting "activated" - and activation is often where our edge lives.

From an RO-DBT lens, when multiple family members (you, your dad) are experiencing the same person as "awful and difficult," this suggests we're looking at a family system under stress rather than just individual pathology.

## Your Edge Opportunity

Here's your growth edge: **Can you get curious about what your sister's behavior might be communicating about her internal experience?**

This doesn't mean excusing harmful behavior - it means practicing the RO-DBT skill of **flexible mind** by considering that all behavior makes sense in context, even when we can't see that context clearly.

## Specific RO-DBT Skills for This Situation

### 1. Self-Enquiry Practice (3-5 minutes)
Right now, sit quietly and ask yourself:
- "What am I defending against when it comes to my sister?"
- "What would it mean about me if I approached her with curiosity instead of judgment?"
- "What am I afraid would happen if I softened my stance even slightly?"

Don't try to answer these quickly. Let the questions work on you.

### 2. Social Signaling Assessment
Notice your facial expressions, tone, and body language when interacting with your sister. Are you unconsciously signaling threat or safety? RO-DBT teaches that we often escalate conflicts through subtle social signals we're not aware of.

### 3. Activating Social Safety
Before your next interaction with her:
- Take 3 slow exhales (longer out-breath than in-breath)
- Soften your facial muscles
- Set an intention to be curious rather than right

## Reframes to Consider

**Instead of:** "She's being awful and difficult"
**Try:** "She's struggling and her coping strategies are creating pain for everyone"

**Instead of:** "Why can't she just be normal?"
**Try:** "What might she need that she doesn't know how to ask for?"

**Instead of:** "This is hopeless"
**Try:** "This is information about our family system under stress"

## Concrete Steps You Can Take

1. **Practice the 24-hour rule**: Before responding to any triggering behavior from your sister, wait 24 hours and do a self-enquiry practice first.

2. **Find one small thing to appreciate**: This week, notice one thing your sister does that isn't awful. Practice radical openness by acknowledging it, even if just to yourself.

3. **Share your struggle (outing yourself)**: Consider telling a trusted friend: "I'm really struggling with how to be a good brother right now." This reduces shame and isolation.

## Supporting Your Dad

Your dad is likely feeling caught between his children. Consider saying something like: "Dad, I know this situation with Emma is hard on you too. I'm working on my own responses, and I want you to know you don't have to fix this or choose sides."

## Managing Your Own Emotional Response

When you feel that familiar surge of frustration with your sister, try this:
1. **Name it**: "I'm getting activated"
2. **Locate it**: "I feel tension in my chest/jaw/shoulders"
3. **Get curious**: "What is this activation trying to protect me from?"
4. **Choose your response**: "How do I want to show up in this moment?"

## Your Self-Enquiry Question for This Week

**"What would it look like to love my sister even when I don't like her behavior?"**

Sit with this for 3-5 minutes daily. Don't try to solve it or feel guilty if the answer isn't immediately clear. The practice is in the asking, not the answering.

Remember: You cannot control your sister's behavior, but you can absolutely influence the family system through your own radical openness and emotional regulation. Sometimes the most rebellious thing we can do in a difficult family dynamic is refuse to participate in the patterns that keep everyone stuck.
