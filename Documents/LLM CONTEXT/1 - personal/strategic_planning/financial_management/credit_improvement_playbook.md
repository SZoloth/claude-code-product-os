# Credit Improvement Playbook

## TLDR
• Pay off high-interest debt first, but don't expect instant credit score jumps.  
• Keep old accounts open to maintain average account age.  
• Request credit line increases to lower utilization.  
• Use secured cards if needed, and pay on time.  
• Don't stress about slow progress—credit repair is a marathon, not a sprint.

## Step-by-Step Credit Improvement Playbook

### 1. Prioritize High-Interest Debt
• Focus on paying off credit cards and loans with the highest interest rates (often 20%+).  
• Aggressive repayment saves you the most money long-term, even if your credit score doesn't jump right away.

### 2. Maintain Old Credit Accounts
• Don't close your oldest credit cards, even if you don't use them much.  
• Closing old accounts lowers your average account age, which can hurt your score.

### 3. Manage Credit Utilization
• Try to keep your credit utilization (balance/limit) below 30% on each card.  
• If possible, call your card issuers and request a credit line increase. This lowers your utilization ratio, which can boost your score.  
• If denied, consider moving to secured cards to rebuild credit.

### 4. Use Secured Credit Cards Wisely
• If you're rebuilding, secured cards are a solid tool. Use them for small purchases and pay off the balance in full each month.  
• Collections accounts are tough, but consistent on-time payments help over time.

### 5. Pay On Time, Every Time
• Payment history is the single biggest factor in your credit score.  
• Set up automatic payments or reminders to avoid missed payments.

### 6. Become an Authorized User (If Possible)
• If you have a trusted family member with a long, positive credit history, ask to be added as an authorized user on their card. This can help your average account age and credit mix.

### 7. Don't Obsess Over Micro-Optimizations
• Don't stress about tiny hacks—focus on the basics: pay on time, keep utilization low, and avoid new hard inquiries unless necessary.

### 8. Monitor Your Credit Regularly
• Use free tools (like Credit Karma or your bank's credit monitoring) to track your score and spot errors.

### 9. Negotiate or Settle Old Debts (If Needed)
• If you have old debts in collections, sometimes settling for less than the full amount is possible. This can help you move forward, but get any agreement in writing.

### 10. Be Patient—Credit Repair Takes Time
• Most people see meaningful improvement in 6–12 months of consistent, positive behavior.  
• Don't get discouraged by slow progress; the system rewards consistency.

## Key Best Practices

• **Financial literacy is a journey**: Don't be afraid to ask questions or seek help.  
• **Avoid closing old accounts**: Age matters.  
• **Secured cards are a good reset**: Especially after collections.  
• **Credit line increases help utilization**: But don't take on new debt.  
• **Stay organized**: Use reminders, spreadsheets, or apps to track payments and balances.

## Specifics from the Conversation

• Closing old accounts lowers your average account age.  
• Secured cards are a good tool for rebuilding.

• Credit repair is slow, but 6–9 months of good behavior can make a big difference.
