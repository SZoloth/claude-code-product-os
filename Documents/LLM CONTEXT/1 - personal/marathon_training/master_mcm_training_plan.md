## Marine Corps Marathon Race Strategy

### Course-Specific Tactics
**Miles 1-3: The Rosslyn Hills**
- Start 15-20 seconds slower than goal pace (9:00-9:05/mile)
- Conserve energy on the gradual climbs
- Don't get caught up in race excitement

**Miles 4-13: Rolling Terrain**
- Settle into goal pace (8:47/mile) by mile 6
- Practice negative splitting: give away 5-10 seconds early, gain back later
- Key landmarks: Lincoln Memorial, Washington Monument

**Miles 14-20: The Flats & "Beat the Bridge"**
- Maintain steady effort through National Mall
- Critical cutoff at mile 20 - stay on pace!
- This is where your marathon pace practice pays off

**Miles 21-26.2: The Finish**
- Dig deep through Capitol Hill area
- Final push with Marine motivation
- Remember: you've practiced this effort in training

### Fueling Strategy
- **Pre-race:** Normal breakfast 3-4 hours before
- **During race:** 
  - Water every 2-3 miles
  - Sports drink alternating with water
  - Gel at miles 8, 14, 18, 22 (practice in long runs)
- **Goal:** 200-250 calories/hour after first hour

## Key Training Principles

### 1. Progressive Overload
- Weekly mileage increases 10-15% most weeks
- Peak mileage of 50 miles (Week 11)
- Built-in recovery weeks every 4th week

### 2. Specificity
- 30-40% of training at or near marathon pace
- Long runs simulate race conditions
- Course-specific hill training in early phases

### 3. Recovery & Adaptation
- Easy runs truly easy (9:30-10:15/mile)
- Recovery weeks reduce volume 25-30%
- Active recovery with cross-training

### 4. Quality Over Quantity
- Two quality sessions per week maximum
- Every workout has a specific purpose
- No "junk miles" - every run has intent

## Injury Prevention & Recovery

### Daily Habits
- **Pre-run:** Dynamic warm-up (leg swings, lunges, etc.)
- **Post-run:** Static stretching, focus on calves, IT band, hips
- **Evening:** Foam rolling 10-15 minutes

### Weekly Requirements
- **Monday:** Complete rest or gentle yoga
- **One day:** Deep tissue massage or foam rolling session
- **Sleep:** 7-9 hours nightly (critical for adaptation)

### Warning Signs to Watch
- Persistent pain lasting >3 days
- Inability to maintain easy pace at prescribed effort
- Unusual fatigue or elevated resting heart rate
- **Action:** Take extra rest day, see medical professional if needed

## Flexibility & Modifications

### If You Miss Workouts
- **Miss a quality day:** Skip it, don't double up
- **Miss easy run:** Add miles to next easy day (max +2 miles)
- **Miss long run:** Move to next available day, reduce by 1-2 miles

### Weather Adaptations
- **Heat:** Slow pace by 20-30 seconds/mile, increase hydration
- **Cold:** Extended warm-up, layer appropriately
- **Rain/ice:** Treadmill or indoor alternative

### Travel Weeks
- Maintain easy runs only
- Skip quality workouts rather than compromise form
- Return to schedule the following week

## Emergency Protocols

### Injury Occurrence
1. **Minor niggles:** Extra rest day, ice, gentle stretching
2. **Persistent pain:** See sports medicine professional immediately
3. **Serious injury:** Reassess race goal, consider deferring

### Illness During Training
- **Head cold:** Easy runs only, no quality work
- **Chest/systemic illness:** Complete rest until 24hrs symptom-free
- **Race week illness:** Reassess participation with medical guidance

### Major Schedule Disruption
- **1 week missed:** Resume at 80% previous week's volume
- **2+ weeks missed:** Reassess race goal with coach/advisor
