Below is a “greatest-hits” cheat-sheet that runners keep returning to when they build—or tweak—a marathon plan.  I’ve grouped the information by widely-used frameworks, then pulled out the rules & evergreen tips they share, and finished with how to make the most of Strava and other modern tools.

⸻

1. Corner-stone Frameworks & What They Emphasise

Framework	Mileage Philosophy	Key Workouts & Structure	Why People Love It
Hal Higdon (Novice → Advanced)	Gradual weekly build with “step-back” recovery weeks; peak long run 20 mi ~3 wks out	1 weekly long run, 1 – 2 quality days (tempo or intervals as level rises), mandatory rest day	Friendly entry point; clearly written schedules; low injury rate for beginners
Jack Daniels Running Formula	Volume tailored to VDOT (fitness) score rather than arbitrary miles	Four paces: Easy (E), Threshold (T), Interval (I), Repetition (R); long run ≤ 2 h 30 m	Science-driven; precise paces keep workouts “honest”
Hansons Marathon Method	45 – 55 mi wk⁻¹ for many athletes, but never a monster 20-miler—long run tops at 16 mi (~25–30 % weekly volume)	3 “Something-Of-Substance” days: speed/strength, marathon-pace tempo, long run; cumulative fatigue is the secret sauce	Teaches holding pace on tired legs—great simulation for miles 20-26
80/20 Running (Fitzgerald)	~80 % of running in low-intensity zones 1-2, 20 % at moderate/hard	Weekly template mixes long easy miles with one tempo & one interval/stride session	Proven to raise aerobic capacity while keeping injury risk low
Pfitzinger & Douglas – “Advanced Marathoning”	Medium-long runs mid-week; peak volume 55–85 mi wk⁻¹; 18- to 12-wk macrocycles	Lactate-threshold (“LT”) runs, VO₂-max intervals, tune-up races; 3-wk taper	Favoured by sub-3-hour hopefuls for its balance of volume & quality
FIRST / “Run Less Run Faster”	Three quality runs (speed, tempo, long) + 2-3 days aggressive cross-training	Precise paces from recent 5 K; bike/row/swim maintain aerobic load	Time-starved athletes who still crave speed work
Maffetone / Low-HR Base	4-6 mo at ≤ (180 – age) HR; nearly all easy mileage	Builds enormous aerobic base & fat-burning; speed only later	Injury-prone or masters runners seeking longevity

2. Universal “Rules of the Road”
	1.	Build volume slowly – classic 10 % rule (no more than ~10 % weekly mileage jump) still guards against overuse injuries.
	2.	Make the long run sacred – 20 – 30 % of weekly distance, cap it at ~3 h (or 16 mi in Hansons). (2025-09-23 note: For sub‑45 mi weeks during peak, a single week may reach ~35–40 % if days before/after are truly easy and you still keep total long‑run time ≤ 3:00.)
	3.	Keep easy days easy – most plans hover around 70–85 % at conversational pace; save the matches for workouts.
	4.	One hard, one moderate, one long: never stack two “red-zone” days back-to-back (workout → recovery jog or rest).
	5.	Three-week taper – cut volume 20 – 30 % each week while retaining a sprinkle of marathon-pace work.
	6.	Fuel early, fuel often – 30–60 g carb h⁻¹ on long runs; practice exactly what you’ll take on race day.
	7.	Strength & mobility 2× week – glute/hip work, core circuits, and calf raises slash injury risk.
	8.	Periodically cut back – every 3–4 wks drop mileage ~25 % to consolidate gains.
	9.	Rotate shoes & surfaces – mixes stress patterns and lets foams rebound.
	10.	Listen to biomarkers – resting HR, HRV, mood, and sleep quality flag over-training sooner than pace.

3. Making Digital Tools (e.g., Strava) Work for You

Strava Feature	How It Helps
Relative Effort / HR-based stress score – converts workouts across sports to a single fatigue metric so you can spot over-reach before an injury	
Fitness & Freshness chart – long-term CTL/ATL style view; pair with resting-HR for smarter recovery calls.	
Route Builder + Segments – design long-run courses with hydration stops, mimic race elevation, and heat-map crowd-sourced pacing.	
Strava Plans & partner workouts (McMillan, Nike, etc.) – plug-and-play calendars that auto-sync to watches; useful for time-crunched athletes.	
Community & Clubs – accountability loops, race simulations, and pacing buddies in virtual events.	

Tip: Export your Strava calendar to Google Cal; treat runs like immovable meetings. It reinforces consistency without draining willpower.

4. Quick-Fire Tips & Tricks
	•	“Start slow to finish strong” – first 3 mi of every long run should feel too easy.
	•	Include strides year-round – 4-6 × 20 s accelerations after easy runs keep neuromuscular snap without extra fatigue.
	•	Use the weather scale – in heat/humidity adjust target pace by ~2–4 % per 10 °F above 60 °F.
	•	Practice race-morning breakfast every weekend so your gut isn’t surprised at mile 0.
	•	Log how you felt not just numbers – future-you will thank past-you when deciphering niggles.
	•	“N + 1 = gear” – have a spare watch charger, extra socks, and a backup fuel packet in your drop bag.

⸻

Bringing It All Together

Choose the framework that matches your life constraints and injury history, then layer on:
	1.	80 % easy / 20 % quality intensity distribution.
	2.	Weekly focal points: one long endurance builder, one strength/speed stimulus, lots of easy connecting tissue.
	3.	Objective monitoring (Strava metrics, HRV) + subjective feel (mood, muscle soreness).
	4.	Consistent nutrition & sleep—because adaptation happens when you’re not running.

Mix and match, stay patient, and you’ll toe the start line fitter, fresher, and more confident than ever.  Happy training! 🏃‍♂️💨
