# Product Leadership: Decision-Making and Influence Skills
## Lesson Plan for Strategic Leadership Development

*Created: July 31, 2025*  
*Context: DreamWorks Animation Digital Backlot project learnings*

---

## **Learning Objectives**
By the end of this lesson plan, you will:
1. Apply structured decision-making frameworks to complex stakeholder environments
2. Execute executive influence strategies that drive organizational change
3. Develop transferable leadership skills for high-stakes product initiatives

---

## **Module 1: Decision-Making Framework Development**

### **1.1 The OODA Loop for Complex Stakeholder Environments**
**Framework**: Observe → Orient → Decide → Act

**Application Example** (DWA Digital Backlot):
- **Observe**: Gather stakeholder perspectives (Tony, Jeff, Cindy conversations)
- **Orient**: Synthesize into frameworks and identify power dynamics  
- **Decide**: Choose influence strategy with key stakeholders
- **Act**: Execute and cycle back to observe results

**Skill Development Exercise**:
- Track your current decision cycles
- Identify where you get stuck (most leaders over-analyze in "Orient")
- Practice faster OODA loops on smaller decisions
- Set timers for each phase to build speed

**Key Insight**: Speed of learning beats perfection of analysis in dynamic environments.

### **1.2 The Cynefin Framework for Strategic Decisions**

**Framework**: Categorize problems to choose appropriate response
- **Simple**: Best practices work
- **Complicated**: Good practices + expertise
- **Complex**: Emergent practices + experimentation
- **Chaotic**: Novel practices + rapid response

**DWA Metrics Example**: 
- **Domain**: Complicated (expertise matters, analysis helps)
- **Strategy**: Leverage expert knowledge (Cindy's PSO background) + proven patterns
- **Avoid**: Over-innovation when good practices exist

**Practice Questions**:
- What domain does my current challenge sit in?
- Am I using the right approach for this domain?
- Who are the relevant experts I should leverage?

### **1.3 Decision Quality Assessment**
**Pre-Decision Checklist**:
- [ ] Have I identified the real decision maker?
- [ ] Do I understand their success criteria?
- [ ] Have I considered 2nd and 3rd order effects?
- [ ] What's my fallback if this doesn't work?

**Post-Decision Review**:
- What did I learn about the decision process?
- Which stakeholder reactions surprised me?
- What would I do differently next time?

## **Module 2: Executive Influence Skills Development**

### **2.1 The Three Languages of Leadership**
Every executive conversation requires fluency in all three:

**Business Language**: ROI, efficiency gains, competitive advantage
- *Keywords*: Revenue impact, cost savings, market position
- *DWA Example*: "TAA reduction directly correlates to production cost savings"

**Operational Language**: Process, systems, risk mitigation
- *Keywords*: Scalability, reliability, process optimization
- *DWA Example*: "Leveraging your PSO expertise to build operational excellence"

**Political Language**: Stakeholder alignment, organizational dynamics  
- *Keywords*: Coalition building, influence mapping, win-win outcomes
- *DWA Example*: "Positioning framework as Randy's success enabler"

**Practice Exercise**:
- Rewrite your next executive presentation in all three languages
- Identify which language each stakeholder prefers
- Practice code-switching between languages in same conversation

### **2.2 The Influence Sequence Model**
**Trust → Credibility → Advocacy**

**Phase 1 - Trust Building**:
- Acknowledge existing work ("building on your foundation")
- Show you understand their challenges
- Demonstrate respect for their expertise
- *Time Investment*: 30-40% of relationship building

**Phase 2 - Credibility Establishment**:
- Present frameworks with clear rationale
- Show comprehensive research and preparation
- Connect to business outcomes they care about
- *Evidence Required*: Data, precedents, expert validation

**Phase 3 - Advocacy Creation**:
- Make them co-creators, not recipients
- Give them wins they can claim
- Create mutual success dependencies
- *Ultimate Goal*: They sell your ideas when you're not in the room

**Influence Strategy Selection Matrix**:

| Stakeholder Power | Stakeholder Interest | Influence Strategy | Tactics |
|-------------------|---------------------|-------------------|---------|
| High Power, High Interest | **Collaborate & Partner** | Co-creation, shared ownership |
| High Power, Low Interest | **Inform & Convince** | Executive briefings, ROI focus |
| Low Power, High Interest | **Involve & Leverage** | Champions, detailed engagement |
| Low Power, Low Interest | **Monitor** | Minimal engagement, status updates |

### **2.3 Executive Presence Development**

**Strategic Thinking Signals**:
- Lead with implications, not features
- Connect tactical decisions to business outcomes  
- Anticipate objections before they're raised
- Reference external validation and industry trends

**Confidence Without Arrogance**:
- "Based on my research..." (not "I think...")
- "What we're seeing across the industry..." (external validation)
- "How does this align with your experience?" (inviting expertise)
- "The data suggests..." (evidence-based positioning)

**Executive Communication Structure**:
1. **Context** (30 seconds): Why this matters now
2. **Insight** (60 seconds): What you've discovered
3. **Implication** (30 seconds): What this means for them
4. **Ask** (30 seconds): Specific decision/input needed

## **Module 3: Concrete Skill-Building Exercises**

### **3.1 Decision Journaling**
**Daily Practice** - After each stakeholder interaction, write:
- What decision did I need to make?
- What framework did I use (or should have used)?
- What information was missing?
- What would I do differently?
- What pattern am I seeing across interactions?

**Weekly Review**:
- Which decisions had the highest impact?
- Where did I rush vs. over-analyze?
- What's my decision-making success rate?

### **3.2 Influence Preparation Ritual**
**Before each executive meeting**, answer:
- What's their biggest fear/concern about this topic?
- What success do they need to achieve?
- How can I make them look good to their stakeholders?
- What's the minimum viable ask that moves us forward?
- What coalition do I need them to join?

**Templates for Preparation**:
```
Stakeholder: [Name]
Power Level: [High/Medium/Low]
Interest Level: [High/Medium/Low]
Success Definition: [What they need to win]
Influence Strategy: [Based on matrix above]
Key Messages: [Tailored to their language preference]
Desired Outcome: [Specific and measurable]
```

### **3.3 Power Mapping Practice**
**Weekly Exercise** - Map the formal vs. informal power structure:
- Who really influences decisions?
- What are the coalition possibilities?
- Where are the hidden vetoes?
- How do relationships affect decisions?

**Power Mapping Template**:
- **Decision Makers**: Who has formal authority?
- **Influencers**: Who do decision makers listen to?
- **Blockers**: Who can say no or slow things down?
- **Champions**: Who actively supports your initiatives?
- **Connectors**: Who bridges different groups/departments?

## **Module 4: Advanced Leadership Mental Models**

### **4.1 Systems Thinking Over Linear Thinking**

**Linear Thinking**: "If we implement metrics, we'll get buy-in"

**Systems Thinking**: "Metrics implementation affects multiple stakeholder relationships, creates new information flows, changes power dynamics, and generates ripple effects that need active management"

**Practice Framework**:
- Map the system: Who are all the affected parties?
- Identify feedback loops: How does change create more change?
- Find leverage points: Where can small changes create big impact?
- Anticipate unintended consequences: What could go wrong?

### **4.2 Playing Chess, Not Checkers**

**Checkers Mindset**: React to immediate moves, focus on individual interactions

**Chess Mindset**: Think 3-4 moves ahead across multiple stakeholders

**DWA Chess Game Example**:
- **Move 1**: Cindy conversation (build alliance)
- **Move 2**: Randy presentation (secure resources)  
- **Move 3**: Implementation support (prove value)
- **Move 4**: Long-term partnership (expand influence)

**Chess Planning Questions**:
- If I succeed with this stakeholder, what becomes possible?
- If I fail, what are my alternative paths?
- How does this move set up future moves?
- What would my opposition do in response?

### **4.3 The Paradox of Control**
**Principle**: The more directly you try to control outcomes, the less influence you often have.

**Instead of controlling outcomes**: Focus on creating conditions for others to make the decisions you want them to make.

**Application Techniques**:
- **Option Architecture**: Present choices where all options serve your goals
- **Success Attribution**: Let others claim credit for your ideas
- **Problem Ownership**: Help them discover problems rather than telling them
- **Solution Co-Creation**: Guide them to your conclusions through questions

## **Module 5: Immediate Application Framework**

### **5.1 Pre-Meeting Preparation Template**
**Decision Frame**: What specific decision do I need from this person?
**Success Metric**: How will I know the meeting worked?
**Influence Strategy**: Which quadrant are they in? (Power/Interest matrix)
**Chess Move**: What does this person need to be successful with their stakeholders?
**Languages**: Which of the three languages (business/operational/political) do they prefer?

### **5.2 During-Meeting Execution**
**Trust Building** (First 30%):
- Acknowledge their expertise and existing work
- Show you understand their challenges
- Demonstrate preparation and respect

**Credibility Building** (Middle 40%):
- Present framework as enhancing their work
- Use their preferred language
- Connect to their success metrics

**Advocacy Creation** (Final 30%):
- Ask for their strategic input
- Make them co-creators
- Define mutual success

### **5.3 Post-Meeting Follow-Through**
**Immediate** (Within 24 hours):
- Send summary with their input highlighted
- Capture lessons learned in decision journal
- Identify what influence techniques worked/didn't work

**Strategic** (Within 1 week):
- Plan next moves in the broader chess game
- Update power map based on new information
- Prepare for ripple effects of any agreements

## **Module 6: Mastery Development Plan**

### **6.1 Skill Assessment Rubric**

**Novice**: Reacts to stakeholder requests without strategic framework
**Competent**: Uses basic influence techniques and structured decision-making
**Proficient**: Integrates multiple frameworks and anticipates stakeholder reactions
**Expert**: Shapes the decision-making environment and builds lasting influence networks
**Master**: Others seek them out for strategic counsel and complex stakeholder navigation

**Self-Assessment Questions**:
- Where am I on this rubric for decision-making?
- Where am I for influence skills?
- What's the next level behavior I need to practice?

### **6.2 30-60-90 Day Development Plan**

**30 Days**: Foundation Building
- Implement decision journaling daily
- Practice influence preparation ritual
- Apply OODA loop to 3 real decisions
- Complete power mapping exercise

**60 Days**: Integration & Refinement  
- Teach these frameworks to a colleague
- Handle a complex stakeholder situation using systematic approach
- Collect feedback on influence effectiveness
- Refine personal frameworks based on results

**90 Days**: Mastery Applications
- Successfully navigate a multi-stakeholder initiative
- Mentor someone else on decision-making
- Develop your own frameworks building on these foundations
- Measure improvement in stakeholder relationship quality

### **6.3 Meta-Skill Development**
The ultimate skill being developed: **Strategic Patience**

Building influence through consistent value delivery rather than attempting to push through on charisma alone.

**Practice Areas**:
- Delayed gratification in stakeholder relationships
- Long-term thinking in immediate tactical situations
- Building systems vs. solving individual problems
- Creating conditions for others' success as path to your own

## **Reflection Questions for Ongoing Development**

1. **Decision-Making**: What decisions am I avoiding? Why?
2. **Influence**: Who do I need to influence that I'm not currently engaging?
3. **Systems**: What unintended consequences am I not anticipating?
4. **Chess**: How many moves ahead am I thinking?
5. **Control**: Where am I trying to control outcomes vs. creating conditions?

**Remember**: These are not just tactics for your current project—they're transferable leadership capabilities that compound over time.
