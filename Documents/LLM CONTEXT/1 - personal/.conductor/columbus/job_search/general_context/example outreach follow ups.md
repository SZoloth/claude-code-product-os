This document helps you with the emails/texts you want to send to:
1. Set up a Listening Tour conversation (reverse exit interview, golden question,
recruiter interview)
2. Send a thank-you note.
Right after the conversation, reach out and thank them. Cite some specific things
they said, and then let them know you’ll keep them updated. See my book
website for some sample thank-you notes (and update notes).
3. Update everyone you’ve spoken to.
Every month or quarter, you should send an email to everyone you have met
with.
First rule: Make your Listening Tour conversations live via phone or in-person (i.e., do
not try to get their feedback asynchronously over email).
Second rule: Always send a follow-up/thank you note. One mistake many job seekers
make is to not send thank-you notes and not update the people they’ve met with.
Updating deepens relationships and strengthens your community of support throughout
your job search and beyond. It’s also part of living a life of service to others.
In addition to the samples below, The Muse has some good suggestions here: A Perfect
Interview Thank You Email (Template + Samples!) | The Muse.
And we strongly recommend you experiment with ChatGPT for drafting your notes.

Listening Tour Outreach
Tip: Create a Calendly account or similar time booking tool to use in your email footer. I found
that people sometimes prefer to use automated options instead of back-and-forth on finding a
time.
Recruiter Outreach Email
Hi [Name],
Hope things are going great for you and [company].
Could we catch up this week or next? Maybe 20-30 min by phone or Zoom.
I’d love your advice and input: I’m at the very beginning of my own job search, but not yet asking
for leads or specific jobs yet.
Instead, I’m asking you to share your hard-won experience to help me understand how the
market might see me today.
Here’s my current LinkedIn: [LinkedIn URL]
I’d specifically like to discuss:
1. In your opinion, what kind of job could you (or another recruiter) place me in tomorrow
with no sweat?
2. What would be a stretch?
3. And, what would you say is definitely out of my league?
I’d be grateful for any time you could share with me - I’ve linked my own calendar picker below,
and would be equally happy to select a time from yours.
– [Your Name]
Phone: (555) 555-5555
Calendar: https://calendly.com/book-time

Reverse Exit and Golden Question
- Reverse Exit - Colder contact-
Hey [x],
It’s been a while since we connected. How have you been? How’s the family? [insert some
observation of recent job/role/etc, like, I see you recently joined company X, how is that going?]
Would you be willing to catch up soon for about an hour? I’d love to hear how things are going
since it’s been a while.
I’m also conducting a “listening tour” before my next career move to make sure I target the right
roles next—establishing candidate market fit. If you wouldn’t mind, I’d love to work in your
perspectives about when we worked together: what went well and what could have been better.
Let me know what you think, I hope to connect soon!
- [yourname]
- Reverse Exit - Warm contact -
I texted or Slacked all of these folks since they were still warm.
Hey [x],
Would you be interested in catching up soon? I really enjoyed partnering with you [context] and
hope we can find opportunities to partner again in the future!
I’m hoping you’d be willing to share your perspectives on the current product landscape. I also
have a pretty large network and might be able to help make some intros for roles you are trying
to fill.
What do you think? If you are interested, feel free to find a time on my calendly, link below. I’m
also willing to shift things around to accommodate your schedule, if needed.
Thanks in advance for considering it and I hope you are well!
Best,
[calendly]

- Text to a former direct report
How's [name of new company] going?
I'm in the early stages of looking for my next opportunity and taking some time thinking about
what I want and don't want in my next job.
I'd love to get your honest feedback on what I did well and what I could have done better when
we worked together.
Could I buy you lunch in the next week or two?
- Text to a coworker
Here’s a text to a coworker that goes for a walk with me along a nearby river a few times a year:
It’s a little on the cold side for a walk, but could we grab coffee some morning?
I’m getting more serious about a job search and spending some time identifying what I want in a
new role and what opportunities there are for me.
I’d love to get your feedback on what you see I do well and what I could do better. I’d also like
your input on a document I’m working on that outlines my must-haves and must-nots for a new
job.
I could do coffee tomorrow or next week Mon-Thu. Any of those work for you?
- Text to a coworker
You have some time in the next few days to catch up? It’s been awhile!
I’m in the early stages of a new job search. I’m doing some research to identify what I want in a
new role and what opportunities there are for me.
I’ve always had a lot of respect for you and would love to hear your thoughts on what I did well
at [company we both worked] and what I could have done better.
My day is pretty open, let me know what would work for you either today or sometime soon.
Thanks, I look forward to catching up!

Senior Leaders
Update note from Sally
Sally is a senior leader at a nonprofit organization who is conducting a Slow Seeker
search.
She spoke to several of her current organization’s Board members as part of her
Listening Tour. This is an update note she sent to one of those Board members.
Note that VERY strong positive response from the Board member.
