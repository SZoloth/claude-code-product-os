# How to Use the Integrated Life Review System

## Overview

This system is designed to help you:
1. See patterns across all life domains (not compartmentalized)
2. Surface strategic avoidance and blind spots
3. Track progress in ways that build self-respect
4. Connect daily actions to long-term vision
5. Leverage positive-sum synergies between domains

**Philosophy**: Integration over optimization. Your life domains reinforce each other when you see the connections.

---

## Weekly Workflow

### Sunday Evening: Review + Planning (90-120 minutes total)

**Preparation** (10 minutes):
- Gather context: Review journal entries from week ([[YYYY-MM-DD]] through [[YYYY-MM-DD]])
- Check daily progress files for the week
- Review training log for marathon progress
- Look at work week-in-review if you did one

**Part 1: Week in Review** (45-60 minutes):
1. **Copy the template**: Duplicate `templates/week-in-review-template.md`
2. **Name it**: `week-in-review/YYYY-MM-DD-week-in-review.md` (Sunday date)
3. **Fill it out sequentially** - don't jump around:
   - Start with Executive Summary (this might be easiest to write last, actually)
   - Go through each domain systematically
   - Integration insights - this is where the magic happens
   - Pattern recognition - be brutally honest
   - Self-respect building - find the evidence
   - Gratitude - acknowledge what was good
4. **Be specific**: Use concrete examples, not generalizations
5. **Be honest**: This is for you - name avoidance, blind spots, struggles
6. **Update dashboard**: Take key metrics and update `life-domains-dashboard.md`

**Part 2: Week Ahead Planning** (45-60 minutes):
1. **Copy the template**: Duplicate `templates/week-ahead-template.md`
2. **Name it**: `week-ahead/YYYY-MM-DD-week-ahead.md` (Monday date)
3. **Start with constraints**: What's fixed this week? (Training, commitments, energy)
4. **Identify Big Three**: These are INTEGRATED priorities, not siloed by domain
   - Ask: "What three things would make this week meaningful?"
   - Ensure at least one addresses strategic avoidance if relevant
5. **Map energy to activities**: Use marathon rest days for high-cognition work
6. **Set accountability**: How will you know if you did what matters?
7. **Address patterns**: Explicitly plan to interrupt recurring blind spots

## Component Use Cases

### Life Domains Dashboard (Update Weekly)

**When to update**: During Sunday evening review, after completing week-in-review

**What to update**:
- Status column for each domain (use current state, not aspirational)
- Trend arrows (↗ ↓ →) based on last 2-3 weeks
- Key metrics (be specific and measurable)
- Recent activity logs (job search, work highlights)
- Integration map (new synergies or conflicts noticed)

**How to use it**:
- Quick reference for current state across all domains
- Pattern spotting: What's trending down? What needs attention?
- Integration mapping: How are domains connected?
- Avoidance tracking: Vision work section is intentionally prominent

**Critical function**: The dashboard makes strategic avoidance VISIBLE. If vision work status hasn't changed in weeks, that's the pattern.

### Week in Review (Every Sunday)

**Purpose**: Reflection and pattern recognition, not just recap

**Key principles**:
1. **Be evidence-based**: Use specific examples from the week
2. **Integration is the point**: Don't just report by domain - show connections
3. **Name patterns**: Call out blind spots and recurring themes
4. **Separate validation from self-respect**: Where did you seek approval vs. build genuine confidence?
5. **Celebrate without qualifying**: "I did X" not "I did X but should have done Y"

**Common pitfalls to avoid**:
- Writing what you "should" say vs. what's true
- Glossing over avoidance patterns
- Focusing only on outcomes, not process and growth
- Forgetting to update the dashboard with insights

**Time-saving tips**:
- Review your journal entries first - you already wrote reflections
- Look at daily progress files - work highlights are already captured
- Check training log - fitness data is already recorded
- Use voice-to-text if writing feels like a barrier

### Week Ahead Planning (Every Sunday)

**Purpose**: Intentional design of upcoming week, not just scheduling

**Key principles**:
1. **Start with energy**: Map cognitive capacity to training schedule
2. **Big Three are integrated**: Not "1 work, 1 personal, 1 training" but three meaningful priorities
3. **Address avoidance**: If something has been postponed for weeks, it goes in Big Three
4. **Schedule specifics**: "Vision work" is not a plan. "Monday 7-8:30am: Draft 5-year vision statement" is a plan.
5. **Set checkpoints**: Mid-week and end-of-week accountability

**The Big Three framework**:
- Each priority should answer: "Why does this matter to who I'm becoming?"
- Each should have: Concrete outcome + time estimate + when it will happen
- At least one should address any strategic avoidance patterns

**Energy mapping is critical**:
- Marathon rest days = high cognitive energy = strategic work, job apps, vision work
- Post-hard workout days = lower cognitive energy = admin, email, lighter tasks
- Don't fight your energy - design around it

**Common pitfalls to avoid**:
- Overcommitting - be realistic about time and energy
- Vague intentions - "make progress on X" vs. specific actions
- Ignoring patterns - if you avoided something last week, address it this week
- Forgetting self-respect connection - ask "Will this build genuine confidence?"

## Integration Best Practices

### Finding Positive-Sum Synergies

**How to identify**:
- Look for skills/mindsets that transfer across domains
- Notice when progress in one area creates energy for another
- Find ways one domain's work can serve another domain's goals

**Examples**:
- Marathon discipline → Job search consistency (showing up when you don't feel like it)
- DWA strategic work → Portfolio case studies (work creates job search materials)
- RO-DBT practice → Rejection handling (emotional regulation serves agency building)
- Relationship quality → Energy for everything (Carter time recharges capacity)

**Action**: In week-ahead planning, explicitly design for synergies

### Avoiding Zero-Sum Thinking

**Watch for these thoughts**:
- "If I focus on marathon training, my job search suffers"
- "Work is taking time away from Carter"
- "Personal growth time is time not spent on career"

**Reframe to positive-sum**:
- "Marathon training builds the discipline I need for consistent job search"
- "Quality Carter time recharges my energy for better work"
- "Personal growth makes me a better partner, professional, and human"

**Action**: When you notice zero-sum thinking, write it down and reframe it

### Surfacing Strategic Avoidance

**The dashboard makes avoidance visible**: Check vision work status. If it hasn't changed in weeks, that's avoidance.

**The review surfaces patterns**: "This week I avoided..." section requires honesty

**The planning addresses it**: If something has been avoided repeatedly, it goes in Big Three

**Key question**: "Is this strategic prioritization or strategic avoidance?"
- **Strategic prioritization**: "I'm not doing X because Y is objectively more important right now and I have a plan for when to do X"
- **Strategic avoidance**: "I'm not doing X because it feels uncomfortable/scary/vulnerable and I keep finding reasons to postpone it"

**Action**: Name avoidance explicitly. Block time. Create accountability.

## Coaching Integration

### Using Coaching Insights in Reviews

**After coaching sessions** (life coach, RO-DBT coach):
- Capture key insights in your journal entry for that day
- Reference coaching themes in weekly review
- Integrate coaching practices into weekly planning

**Pattern recognition from coaches**:
- Life coach themes: Strategic avoidance, orchestrator vs. outcome owner, over-accommodation
- RO-DBT themes: Anxiety-comparison loop, jealousy as signal, inhibited expression
- These should appear in your pattern recognition section

**Accountability to coaches**:
- Share progress on commitments made during coaching
- Bring weekly reviews to coaching sessions if helpful
- Use coaching to calibrate: Is this avoidance or prioritization?

### Self-Coaching Between Sessions

**Use the framework even when not in active coaching**:
- Ask yourself: "What would my life coach say about this pattern?"
- Apply RO-DBT lens: "What is this emotion trying to teach me?"
- Use the questions from coaching analysis at the beginning of each review

## Metrics That Matter

### Measure What Builds Self-Respect

**Don't just track outputs** (miles run, applications sent, hours worked)
**Also track inputs** (discipline shown, courage practiced, alignment with values)

**Examples of self-respect metrics**:
- Times I did the hard thing even when I didn't feel like it
- Moments I chose values-aligned action over external approval
- Instances I practiced vulnerability or grew capability
- Evidence I'm becoming someone I respect

### Avoid Vanity Metrics

**Vanity metrics** feel good but don't indicate real progress:
- Social media likes/follows (external validation)
- Busy-ness without outcomes (activity ≠ progress)
- Comparing yourself to others (maladaptive social comparison)

**Real metrics** show actual capability and character growth:
- Job search: Quality of conversations, not just quantity of applications
- Training: Consistent execution of plan, not just fastest times
- Work: Outcomes owned, not just orchestration performed
- Personal: Vulnerability practiced, not just insights gained

## Weekly Cadence Specifics

### Recommended Timing

**Sunday Evening** (Ideal time for most people):
- Review: 6:00-7:00 PM (45-60 min)
- Break: Dinner, walk, transition (30-45 min)
- Planning: 8:00-9:00 PM (45-60 min)
- Total: 90-120 minutes including break

**Why Sunday evening works**:
- Week is fresh in mind for review
- Weekend energy for reflection
- Sets intention for Monday morning
- Aligns with "Sunday evening reset" you already do

**If Sunday doesn't work**: Friday evening review + Sunday evening planning
- Allows processing time between reflection and planning
- Still maintains weekly cadence

### Mid-Week Checkpoint (Wednesday)

**Quick check** (15-20 minutes):
- Are Big Three progressing? If not, adjust.
- Is sleep quality on track? (Foundation for everything)
- Did any blind spots show up? Course-correct now, not Sunday.

**Don't skip this**: Mid-week adjustments prevent "Oh shit, I didn't do anything" Sundays

## Common Challenges & Solutions

### Challenge: "This takes too long"

**Solution 1**: You're already doing the thinking in journals and daily progress - this just organizes it
**Solution 2**: Use voice-to-text for faster capture
**Solution 3**: Start with shorter reviews, build to full template over time
**Solution 4**: The time spent here saves time in confusion and misalignment

### Challenge: "I don't know what to write"

**Solution**: Start with the dashboard status update. Then answer each template question sequentially. Don't try to make it perfect.

**Key insight**: The questions are designed to surface what you need to see. If you're stuck, that's often because you're avoiding something.

### Challenge: "I keep avoiding the strategic stuff"

**Solution**: That's THE POINT of the system - to make avoidance visible.
**Action**: Put the avoided work in Big Three. Block time. Tell someone. Do it first on Monday.
**Remember**: You wouldn't avoid it if it wasn't important or uncomfortable.

### Challenge: "I feel bad when I see I didn't do what I planned"

**Reframe**: The system isn't about perfection, it's about learning.
**Questions to ask**:
- Was the plan realistic? If not, adjust next week.
- What got in the way? Pattern or one-time?
- Did I do something more important? If so, that's good prioritization.
- Did I avoid because it was uncomfortable? If so, that's information.

**Remember**: Self-respect comes from being honest and adjusting, not from perfection.

### Challenge: "My domains feel siloed, not integrated"

**Solution**: Force integration in the review:
- "How did training support job search this week?" (Even if not obvious)
- "How did Carter time recharge me for work?" (Find the connection)
- "How did work strategic thinking help with personal growth?" (There's always a link)

**Key insight**: Integration is a practice. You're training yourself to see connections.

## Evolution of the System

### Month 1: Build the Habit

**Focus**: Consistency over completeness
- Do the Sunday evening review every week, even if brief
- Complete the dashboard update
- Don't worry about perfect answers

### Month 2: Deepen the Practice

**Focus**: Pattern recognition
- Start to see recurring themes across weeks
- Make connections between coaching insights and weekly patterns
- Identify which sections are most valuable to you

### Month 3: Customize and Optimize

**Focus**: Make it yours
- Adjust template sections to your needs
- Add domain-specific tracking that matters to you
- Find the right balance of detail vs. time investment

### Quarterly: Strategic Integration

**Focus**: Connect to longer-term vision
- Review 12 weeks of reviews to see larger patterns
- Update 90-day cycles based on weekly learnings
- Refine 2025-2030 vision based on what you're learning about yourself

## Success Indicators

**You'll know this system is working when**:
1. You can see patterns across weeks and months
2. Strategic avoidance becomes immediately visible (and you address it)
3. You celebrate progress without needing external prompting
4. Decisions become values-aligned rather than fear-driven
5. You feel integrated across life domains, not compartmentalized
6. Coaching insights integrate naturally into your planning

**The ultimate measure**:
Are you building self-respect through evidence of aligned action?
