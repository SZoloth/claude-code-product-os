## RO-DBT Therapy Coach Response - Anxiety & Jealousy/Envy Work

Hello Sam. Thank you for reaching out and being willing to engage with these difficult feelings. It takes courage to turn toward anxiety and jealousy/envy rather than away from them - that willingness itself is an act of radical openness.

Let me help you explore what might be happening and guide you through some RO-DBT practices that could support you right now.

### Understanding What You Might Be Experiencing

**Anxiety and jealousy/envy often travel together in overcontrolled individuals**, and there are some important patterns to notice:

**The Anxiety-Comparison Loop**: Anxiety can intensify when we perceive threats to our status, competence, or belonging. Jealousy/envy often signals that we're comparing ourselves to others and feeling we're falling short in some way that matters deeply to us. For overcontrolled folks, this can trigger:
- Perfectionism ("I should be doing better")
- Rigid self-standards ("I'm failing at what I'm supposed to be")
- Social comparison and threat to belonging ("I'm being left behind")

**The Overcontrol Connection**: In RO-DBT terms, jealousy/envy can reflect:
- **Inhibited emotional expression** - difficulty letting others know we're struggling or want support
- **High social comparison** - constantly measuring ourselves against others' achievements
- **Low reward sensitivity** - difficulty celebrating our own accomplishments
- **Fear of loss of control** - anxiety that others' success somehow threatens our position or worth

### Self-Enquiry Questions

Take 3-5 minutes with these. Remember: the goal is to **find good questions, not quick answers**. Notice where you feel tension or want to move away - that's your "edge."

**Finding Your Body's Signal**
- Where in your body do you feel the anxiety right now? What's the sensation like?
- When you think about what's triggering the jealousy/envy, what happens in your chest, throat, or stomach?
- What does your body want to do right now? (Tense up? Move away? Distract?)

**Finding the Edge**
- What am I most afraid others would think if they knew I was feeling jealous/envious?
- What does this jealousy/envy say about what I deeply value or want for myself?
- What am I comparing? (Skills? Success? Relationships? Recognition? Freedom?)
- If this jealousy/envy is pointing toward something I want - what's getting in the way of pursuing it?

**Exploring the Overcontrol Pattern**
- Am I holding myself to a standard that's unrealistic or unnecessarily rigid?
- Have I been inhibiting expressing my own needs, wants, or struggles?
- What would it mean to celebrate someone else's success while also acknowledging my own desires?
- What am I trying to control that might not be controllable?

**The Vulnerability Question (This is likely close to your edge)**
- What would I need to admit to myself or others if I allowed myself to fully feel what's underneath this jealousy/envy?

### RO-DBT Skills for Working With These Emotions

**1. Self-Enquiry Practice (Start Here - 3-5 minutes)**

Right now, turn toward the feelings rather than solving them:

**Steps:**
1. Find a quiet space. Set a timer for 3-5 minutes.
2. Name what you're feeling: "I'm noticing anxiety. I'm noticing jealousy/envy."
3. Ask yourself: "What is this feeling trying to teach me?"
4. Notice your urge to:
   - Find quick solutions
   - Dismiss the feelings
   - Compare more intensely
   - Distract yourself
5. Each time you notice the urge, gently return to: "What can I learn here?"
6. End by asking: "What would openness to this experience look like?"

**Key reminder**: You're not trying to feel better or solve the problem. You're practicing **being with** the discomfort.

**2. Activating Social Safety (For Anxiety)**

When anxious, your threat system is activated. This physiological practice can help:

**The Half-Smile + Shoulders Down:**
- Relax your face muscles, especially around eyes and forehead
- Create a slight upward turn at the corners of your mouth (genuine or not - physiology matters)
- Drop your shoulders away from your ears
- Breathe slowly: 4 counts in through nose, 6 counts out through mouth
- Repeat for 2-3 minutes

This signals to your nervous system: "I'm safe enough to be open."

**3. Outing Yourself Practice (For Jealousy/Envy)**

This is likely at your edge, but it's powerful:

**Mild version**: Share your feelings with one trusted person, using vulnerability:
- "I'm noticing I'm feeling envious about [situation], and I'm uncomfortable admitting that."
- "I realized I've been comparing myself to [person] and feeling inadequate."

**Why this matters**: Jealousy/envy thrives in secrecy and shame. Overcontrolled people often hide these "unacceptable" emotions, which increases isolation and reinforces the feeling that we're alone in our struggles.

**The key**: Choose someone who can receive this without judgment. The goal is to practice showing your authentic emotional experience, including the "personality quirks" you'd normally hide.

**4. Flexible Mind VARIES (For Breaking the Comparison Loop)**

Do something **novel and nonproductive** to interrupt rigid thought patterns:

Examples:
- Take a different route on a walk and notice 5 new things
- Listen to music you'd normally never choose
- Eat something in a completely different order than usual
- Wear something slightly outside your normal style
- Reach out to someone unexpected just to say hello

**Why**: When we're anxious and comparing, we get rigid. Novel behavior loosens cognitive inflexibility.

**5. Loving-Kindness Toward Your Difficult Emotions**

Try this short practice:

Place your hand on your heart and say:
- "It makes sense that I'm feeling anxious and envious right now."
- "These feelings are pointing toward something I care about."
- "I can be kind to myself while feeling these difficult emotions."
- "May I learn what I need to learn from this experience."
- "May I be open to growth, even when it's uncomfortable."

### RO-DBT Concepts Relevant to Jealousy/Envy

**Maladaptive Social Comparison**
Overcontrolled people tend to engage in frequent upward social comparison ("they're doing better than me") which:
- Activates threat responses
- Reduces reward sensitivity to our own accomplishments
- Increases feelings of inadequacy
- Feeds perfectionism

**The RO-DBT lens**: This isn't a character flaw - it's a learned pattern that once helped you (perhaps by motivating achievement or maintaining vigilance). But now it might be costing you connection and contentment.

**Low Reward Sensitivity**
You might notice it's easier to spot what others have that you lack than to celebrate what you've accomplished. This is characteristic of overcontrol - the brain's reward system is dampened.

**Practice**: Can you name three things you've done well recently, even small things? Notice how hard or easy that is.

**The Tribe Matters Principle**
Jealousy/envy often signals: "I'm afraid I don't belong" or "I'm afraid I'm being left behind by my tribe." This is fundamentally about connection and belonging.

**Question to explore**: Is this jealousy/envy trying to tell you something about:
- Wanting deeper connection?
- Feeling isolated in your struggles?
- Needing to share your own vulnerabilities?
- Wanting recognition or celebration from others?

**Inhibited Emotional Expression**
If you're feeling envious, have you been able to express:
- Your own needs and wants?
- Your own achievements?
- Times you've struggled?
- Times you've wanted support or celebration?

Overcontrolled people often inhibit expression of needs and vulnerabilities, then feel resentful or envious when others seem to get what they want more easily. But often, others are simply expressing their needs more openly.
